Purpose and scope

Enable AI through proportionate controls

This procedure applies to employees, contractors, business units and administrators using Microsoft 365, Copilot, approved AI applications, SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams, Exchange Online or connected business data.

Approved services

Use named work accounts and approved enterprise AI services. Never use personal accounts for company information.

Least privilege

AI may access only information the user is already authorised to access.

Human accountability

AI recommends. Accountable people decide, review and record consequential outcomes.

Operating procedure

AI governance lifecycle

Open each step for the required action. Start with registration and continue through monitoring and incident response.

Register and classify the use case Business Owner
  • Record the owner, users, data sources, intended outcome and affected people.
  • Classify risk as Low, Medium or High.
  • Identify data as Public, Internal, Confidential or Restricted.
  • Obtain the approval appropriate to the risk.
Confirm the approved service Technology Lead
  • Verify the service is approved and uses named work accounts.
  • Review terms, locations, retention, subprocessors, encryption, deletion, export and model-training use.
  • Reject services that cannot provide clear security and privacy answers.
Prepare the Microsoft 365 environment Administrator
  • Enforce MFA and suitable Conditional Access.
  • Review SharePoint, Teams, Groups and OneDrive sharing.
  • Remove broad links, dormant guests and unnecessary permissions.
  • Configure sensitivity labels, retention, DLP and audit logging.
Control data supplied to AI User and Business Owner

Use Public data in approved tools. Internal data requires approved enterprise AI. Confidential data requires an approved use case and safeguards. Restricted data requires documented justification and privacy approval.

Review AI outputs Business Owner
  • Check accuracy, completeness, bias, confidentiality and intellectual-property concerns.
  • Verify important claims against authoritative sources.
  • Require human approval for external and high-impact outputs.
Approve and deploy Approver and Technology Lead
  • Record approval, conditions, residual risk and review date.
  • Assign licences only to trained authorised users.
  • Start with a controlled pilot before wider deployment.
Monitor and reassess Technology Lead and Privacy Officer
  • Review audit events, sensitive-data interactions, sharing activity and errors.
  • Reassess when the model, data, integration, audience, purpose or impact changes.
  • Suspend access if controls fail.
Respond to an AI incident User, Technology Lead and Privacy Officer
  1. Stop activity and preserve records.
  2. Report and contain the incident.
  3. Determine affected data and people.
  4. Follow privacy and security notification processes.
  5. Correct controls before resuming service.

Acceptable use

Clear boundaries for employees

Permitted

  • Use approved AI tools for legitimate business purposes.
  • Use named organisational accounts.
  • Verify outputs before relying on them.
  • Report disclosure, harmful output or unauthorised access.

Prohibited

  • No confidential or personal data in unapproved public AI.
  • No personal AI accounts for company work.
  • No bypassing DLP, labels, retention or access controls.
  • No unapproved plugins, agents, connectors or data sources.
  • No final consequential decisions by AI.

Execution

90-day management action plan

Days 1–30

  • Appoint accountable executive and privacy lead.
  • Inventory AI tools and stop unapproved sensitive use.
  • Publish acceptable-use rules.

Days 31–60

  • Build the use-case register and classify risks.
  • Review permissions and vendor terms.
  • Configure priority sensitivity and DLP controls.

Days 61–90

  • Train users and approvers.
  • Pilot Copilot with a controlled group.
  • Test audit and incident procedures, then remediate before wider deployment.
